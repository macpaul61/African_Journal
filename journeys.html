Wildlife safaris

Where the story begins

Fun fact

Serengeti National Park

“Endless plains” in the Maasai language

Every year around two million wildebeest, zebra and gazelle cross these plains in the Great Migration, one of the largest movements of land mammals on Earth. It has run, largely unbroken, for longer than the national park itself has existed.

Fun fact

Ngorongoro Crater

The world's largest intact volcanic caldera

Roughly two to three million years ago a volcano here erupted and its cone collapsed inward, leaving a bowl 19 kilometres wide. About 25,000 large animals live inside it year-round, rarely needing to leave.

Fun fact

Home to Tanzania's biggest elephant herds

Home to Tanzania's biggest elephant herds

Ancient baobab trees, some over a thousand years old, stand across Tarangire's grasslands. In the dry season, elephant herds gather here in numbers rarely matched anywhere else in northern Tanzania.

Fun fact

Famous for lions that climb trees

Famous for lions that climb trees

Most lions never bother climbing, but Lake Manyara's are a well-known exception, often found draped along acacia branches. The lake itself can turn pink with thousands of flamingos.

Mountains & hiking

Africa's roof, and beyond

Fun fact

Mount Kilimanjaro

Africa's highest point, and its tallest free-standing mountain

At 5,895 metres, Kilimanjaro rises straight from the plains with no mountain range around it. Its summit, Uhuru Peak, means “freedom peak” in Swahili, and still carries glacial ice within a few degrees of the equator.

Fun fact

Tanzania's second-highest peak, and Kilimanjaro's quiet rehearsal

Tanzania's second-highest peak, and Kilimanjaro's quiet rehearsal

Mount Meru is a still-active volcano rising above Arusha National Park. Many climbers summit it first, both to acclimatise and because its narrow ridge walk rivals Kilimanjaro's views.

← Back
Fun fact

The only volcano on Earth that erupts cold, black lava

The only volcano on Earth that erupts cold, black lava

Maasai call it Ol Doinyo Lengai, the Mountain of God. Its lava, rich in natrocarbonatite, erupts almost black and far cooler than typical lava, then turns chalky white within days of touching the air.

Fun fact

Forest trails between the mountains and the plains

Forest trails between the mountains and the plains

Between the peaks and the savannah lie quieter forest walks and crater rims, where colobus monkeys and forest birdsong replace the open horizon for a while.

Waterfalls & nature

Where the mountains breathe out

Fun fact

An 80-metre waterfall in Chagga farming country

An 80-metre waterfall in Chagga farming country

Materuni's falls drop through coffee and banana terraces on Kilimanjaro's lower slopes. Local Chagga families still roast coffee here the traditional way, from picking the cherries to grinding the beans by hand.

Fun fact

Warm spring water beneath fig trees

Warm spring water beneath fig trees

Kikuletwa and Chemka's hot springs sit in a pocket of forest fed by underground volcanic warmth, a favoured stop to unwind after a climb or a long safari day.

Fun fact

A lake so caustic it can calcify what touches it

A lake so caustic it can calcify what touches it

Lake Natron's high alkalinity comes from the volcanic minerals draining into it. Remarkably, it is also the only regular breeding site in East Africa for the lesser flamingo, which thrives where almost nothing else can.

Fun fact

A crater lake near Arusha, calm and little-visited

A crater lake near Arusha, calm and little-visited

Lake Duluti fills an old volcanic crater and is ringed by forest, a quiet half-day away from the safari circuit.

Culture & local life

Tanzania beyond the wildlife

Fun fact

Semi-nomadic pastoralists whose life still centres on cattle

Semi-nomadic pastoralists whose life still centres on cattle

The Maasai are known across East Africa for their beadwork, red shuka cloth, and the adumu, a jumping dance performed at ceremonies. Cattle remain both livelihood and cultural measure of wealth.

← Back
Fun fact

Kilimanjaro's farming people, and Tanzania's coffee pioneers

Kilimanjaro's farming people, and Tanzania's coffee pioneers

The Chagga have farmed Kilimanjaro's fertile southern slopes for centuries, organised historically into small chiefdoms. Coffee and banana beer, brewed the traditional way, remain central to Chagga hospitality.

← Back
Fun fact

From cherry to cup, on the mountain that grows it

From cherry to cup, on the mountain that grows it

Arabica coffee has grown on Kilimanjaro's slopes since German colonial planting in the 1890s, and Chagga farmers still walk visitors through the whole process by hand.

Fun fact

Local markets

Where Tanzania actually shops

Markets like Darajani in Stone Town or Kariakoo in Dar es Salaam are a sensory crash course in Tanzanian daily life, spices, fish, fruit and haggling included.

History & heritage

Layers older than memory

Fun fact

Often called the Cradle of Mankind

Often called the Cradle of Mankind

Olduvai Gorge has yielded some of the oldest hominid fossils and stone tools ever found, close to two million years old, reshaping what we know about human origins.

← Back
Fun fact

Stone Town, Zanzibar

A UNESCO World Heritage trading town

Centuries of Arab, Persian, Indian and European trade layered into one townscape, best known for its elaborately carved wooden doors, no two quite alike.

← Back
Fun fact

Once East Africa's largest caravan port

Once East Africa's largest caravan port

Bagamoyo's name is said to mean “lay down your heart”, a reference to its grim history as a major departure point of the East African slave and ivory trade before later becoming German East Africa's first colonial capital.

← Back
Fun fact

Where the fossils and the history are kept

Where the fossils and the history are kept

National museums in Dar es Salaam and Arusha hold original hominid fossils, colonial-era archives and cultural collections that give context to everywhere else on this list.

Beach & islands

Where the journey slows down

Fun fact

Zanzibar

The Spice Island

Cloves, nutmeg, cinnamon and pepper built Zanzibar's economy for centuries, and spice-farm tours are still one of the best ways to understand the island beyond its beaches.

← Back
Fun fact

Dhow sailing

A sailing tradition older than compass and chart

Wooden dhows have carried people and cargo along this coast for well over a thousand years, riding the monsoon winds between Africa, Arabia and India.

← Back
Fun fact

Mafia Island, Zanzibar's quieter sibling

Mafia Island, Zanzibar's quieter sibling

Part of a marine park protecting whale sharks and coral reefs, Mafia Island trades Zanzibar's crowds for some of the East African coast's best diving.

Fun fact

Dar es Salaam's coastline, the mainland's edge of the ocean

Dar es Salaam's coastline, the mainland's edge of the ocean

Tanzania's largest city sits right on the Indian Ocean, giving a coastal counterpoint before or after a journey inland.
